About the Conference
Welcome to MWVRC 2026

The Mineral Waste Valorization Research Conference 2026 (MWVRC 2026) is a one-day hybrid research conference bringing together postgraduate researchers, academic staff, engineers, industry professionals and policymakers to address the challenge of mineral and industrial waste management in Ghana and beyond.

Organised by the Department of Materials and Metallurgical Engineering at KNUST in partnership with the Organisation for Women in Science for the Developing World (OWSD), this conference is a direct output of the OWSD Early Career Fellowship of Dr. Bennetta Koomson.

About
Conference Overview
Background, theme, objectives and host institution

The Mineral Waste Valorization Research Conference 2026 (MWVRC 2026) is organised by the Department of Materials and Metallurgical Engineering at KNUST, Kumasi, Ghana, in partnership with the Organisation for Women in Science for the Developing World (OWSD).

What is mineral waste? Mineral waste refers to solid, liquid or semi-solid residues generated during the extraction, processing and beneficiation of mineral resources — including mine tailings, slag, waste rock, process effluents and by-products from metallurgical operations.

Ghana's mining sector generates significant volumes of mineral and industrial waste that is poorly managed, underutilised and environmentally costly. This conference fills the gap by bringing engineers, researchers, industry players and policymakers together on a dedicated platform.

The conference is a direct output of the OWSD Early Career Fellowship of Dr. Bennetta Koomson (OWSD Fellow since 2023), whose fellowship is focused on mineral waste valorization research.

Conference Theme

"Valorizing Mineral Waste through Engineering Innovations and Policy Advocacy"

Conference Objectives

  • Provide a platform for sharing research on mineral waste valorization in Ghana and Africa
  • Bridge the gap between academic research, industry practice and policy
  • Foster collaboration between KNUST researchers, industry professionals and government stakeholders
  • Showcase postgraduate research and emerging innovations in waste valorization
  • Contribute to the publication pathway through the JUST journal

Industry Panel
Panel Discussion
25 June 2026 · KNUST, Kumasi, Ghana · Moderated by Prof. Kwadwo Mensah Darkwa
"Valorizing Mineral Waste through Engineering and Policy Innovation"
Moderated by Prof. Kwadwo Mensah Darkwa
01
SGS
Bridging Research, Industry, and Impact
Ghanaian universities and research institutions have generated significant knowledge on mine tailings, waste rock, slags, and metallurgical by-products, yet few of these innovations have reached commercial implementation. What are the key barriers preventing the transition from research to industry adoption, and how can stakeholders work together to convert mineral waste research into scalable economic and environmental solutions?
02
ALS
Characterization as the Foundation of Valorization
Many mineral waste streams remain underutilized because their resource potential is not fully understood. Given the expertise of laboratories, industry, and academia, how can Ghana strengthen mineral waste characterization and data-driven decision-making to unlock value from mine tailings, waste rock, and processing residues?
03
EPA
Policy and Regulatory Transformation
Current regulations often focus on waste containment and environmental compliance rather than resource recovery. What policy, regulatory, and institutional reforms are needed to incentivize mineral waste valorization, discourage environmentally harmful disposal practices, and promote a circular economy within Ghana's mining sector?
04
Intertek
Financing and Commercialization of Innovation
Many promising mineral waste valorization technologies fail to progress beyond pilot studies due to funding and investment challenges. What financing models, public-private partnerships, and investment mechanisms are required to accelerate the commercialization of mineral waste valorization technologies in Ghana and across Africa?
05
ICMS
Building Ghana's Vision for Mineral Waste Valorization
Looking ahead to 2035, what would success look like for Ghana in transforming mineral waste from an environmental liability into a strategic resource, and what single action should government, industry, academia, and regulators prioritize today to achieve that vision?

Best Poster Presentation
Awarded to the researcher who demonstrates outstanding quality, clarity, and impact in their poster presentation during the exhibition session.

  • Clarity and visual quality of the poster
  • Ability to communicate findings to a multidisciplinary audience

Best Oral Presentation
Awarded to the presenter who delivers the most compelling, well-structured, and impactful oral presentation in the technical breakout sessions.

  • Clarity, structure, and delivery of the talk
